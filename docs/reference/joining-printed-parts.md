# Joining printed parts

Reference for joining separately printed pieces: methods, design numbers and tool support.

## Joining methods

"In plane" is load along a flat piece's face, such as strap tension pulling two pieces apart. "Out of plane" is load that lifts or peels one piece off the other. Ratings marked (inference) come from the joint's geometry, not a source.

- **Sliding dovetail, slide axis along the seam.** In plane: resists pull-apart across the seam, free along the slide axis unless tapered or stopped; out of plane: resists, since the tail's profile runs through the thickness, so it needs a thick enough piece (inference). Comes apart by sliding. No hardware. Tolerance: interlocking joints need "a more accurate printer" and allowance for shrinkage ([DigiKey](https://www.digikey.com/en/maker/blogs/2024/how-to-split-and-join-large-3d-prints)); a tapered dovetail "assembles without binding until it is fully closed, and then wedges tightly" ([BOSL2](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/joiners.scad#module-dovetail)). Forum: one user found every Bambu Studio dovetail too tight after changing tolerances (December 2024, no reply) ([Bambu forum](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/dovetails-in-bambu-studio/123308)).
- **Jigsaw or puzzle tab** (a dovetail whose slide axis is normal to the face). In plane: resists pull-apart; out of plane: none, a piece lifts straight off unless glued, pinned or backed (inference). Comes apart. No hardware. Prints flat with no overhang (inference). Tolerance: one maker settled on 0.2 mm between teeth of their "jigsaw-like dovetail joints" for a strong glued joint ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/tag/large-print), blog). A 2026 report titled "C-Shaped Joints Outperform Dovetails in PLA Prints" says "the shape and size of a connection makes a big difference" ([All3DP](https://all3dp.com/4/ditch-the-glue-researchers-find-c-shaped-joints-outperform-dovetails-in-pla-prints/)); the rest is paywalled, so its numbers: not found.
- **Cantilever snap-fit.** Strength depends on which way the hook faces; the beam should deflect only during assembly, not while engaged in service ([Hubs](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-snap-fit-joints-3d-printing)), so strap tension should press on the hook face, not bend the beam (inference). Comes apart if the return angle is under 90°; a 90° return "can never be disassembled" without a release window ([Fictiv](https://www.fictiv.com/articles/how-to-design-snap-fit-components)). No hardware. Tolerance: sensitive, since PLA allows only small undercuts (see design numbers). Hubs calls PLA "less suitable" for snaps ([Hubs](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-snap-fit-joints-3d-printing)).
- **Printed pins or filament pins.** In plane: pins take shear across the seam; out of plane: friction only unless glued (inference). Comes apart if not glued. Hardware: 1.75 mm filament, or a printed pin (a slicer "Dowel" prints as a separate piece ([Prusa](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/cut-tool_1779))). Tolerance: forgiving, since a hole can be drilled to size. Forum: one user makes "1.75 mm holes on both sides and use[s] a piece of filament", another oversizes holes "0.15mm-ish for interference fit parts" ([Prusa forum](https://forum.prusa3d.com/forum/original-prusa-i3-mk3s-mk3-hardware-firmware-and-software-help/adding-alignment-pins-in-slicr), 2020). A scenery maker drills 2 mm holes for 1.75 mm filament, glues the pins and leaves 3–4 mm protruding ([Printable Scenery](https://www.printablescenery.com/2025/09/05/using-filament-in-the-guide-holes/), blog). Pins need parts "thick enough" and accurate small diameters ([DigiKey](https://www.digikey.com/en/maker/blogs/2024/how-to-split-and-join-large-3d-prints)).
- **Dowels (steel, brass).** Like pins, stiffer. Forum: 2 mm steel roll pins in holes printed at about 2.1 mm and drilled to 2 mm, because "printed holes usually turn out smaller" ([Prusa forum](https://forum.prusa3d.com/forum/original-prusa-i3-mk3s-mk3-hardware-firmware-and-software-help/adding-alignment-pins-in-slicr)). Brass pins in drilled holes for alignment ([Mandarin3D](https://mandarin3d.com/blog/3d-printing-for-cosplay-armor-props-and-accessories), vendor blog).
- **Magnets.** In plane: weak; magnet pairs resist sliding with about half their pull force (47–70% measured, 40–75% simulated, friction ignored) ([K&J Magnetics](https://www.kjmagnetics.com/blog/magnet-shear-force)), and a cosplay maker found magnet latches "weakest horizontally, consider mounting so parts cannot slide apart" ([MyMiniFactory](https://www.myminifactory.com/object/3d-print-125951)). Out of plane: their strongest direction. Comes apart quickly. Hardware: magnets and glue (that maker superglues 8 × 3 mm magnets into pockets). Tolerance: forgiving (inference).
- **Screws with heat-set inserts or nuts.** Strong both ways and easy to take apart ([DigiKey](https://www.digikey.com/en/maker/blogs/2024/how-to-split-and-join-large-3d-prints)). Hardware: screws, plus nuts or inserts and a soldering iron 10–20 °C above print temperature (about 225 °C for PLA); blind holes about 1 mm deeper than the insert ([CNC Kitchen](https://cnckitchen.com/blog/tips-and-tricks-for-heat-set-inserts)). CNC Kitchen's hole diameters are in an image on that page: not found in text. Walls around bores must be thick enough ([DigiKey](https://www.digikey.com/en/maker/blogs/2024/how-to-split-and-join-large-3d-prints)), which on a thin piece means local bosses (inference). Tolerance: forgiving with clearance holes (inference).
- **Separate clips.** Printed parts that bridge the seam, such as BOSL2's double `rabbit_clip()` or `snap_pin()` (see Tool support). `rabbit_clip()` can be made "to insert and release easily, or to hold much better", or to lock ([BOSL2 source](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/blob/master/joiners.scad)).
- **Glue.** Strong in shear, weakest in peel (inference). Permanent: glued butt joints are "typically not possible" to take apart, but forgive print accuracy ([DigiKey](https://www.digikey.com/en/maker/blogs/2024/how-to-split-and-join-large-3d-prints)). For PLA: CA gel (fast; "the bond can be brittle"), two-part epoxy (strongest, 12–24 h full cure), solvent glues such as 3D Gloop! ([Snapmaker](https://www.snapmaker.com/blog/best-glue-for-pla-3d-prints/), vendor blog); heat-welding PLA seams with a soldering iron ([Mandarin3D](https://mandarin3d.com/blog/3d-printing-for-cosplay-armor-props-and-accessories), vendor blog). Conflict: Snapmaker rates CA gel "best overall"; Mandarin3D says CA "can leave white residue" and doesn't recommend it for final assembly.

## Design numbers

Filament: Bambu Lab PLA Pure. Most fit numbers below come from blogs and forums, none measured on PLA Pure or an X2D. "Unstated" means the source doesn't say per side or total.

Clearances

- BOSL2 `$slop` defaults to 0. Its calibration print cuts holes in 0.05 mm steps; the smallest hole a plug enters without much force gives the printer's value ([BOSL2](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/constants.scad#constant-slop)). In `dovetail()` it is per side, "measured normal to the mating faces" ([BOSL2](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/joiners.scad#module-dovetail)); for `partition_cut_mask()` it "should probably be smaller than 0.2" ([BOSL2](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/partitions.scad#section-legacy-partitioning)).
- Bambu Studio connectors: Tolerance defaults to 0.1 mm ([CutUtils.hpp](https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/blob/v02.08.02.61/src/libslic3r/CutUtils.hpp)); the code adds it to the hole's radius, so it is per side (my reading of `ModelVolume::apply_tolerance` in [Model.cpp](https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/blob/v02.08.02.61/src/libslic3r/Model.cpp)). A forum poster gave the same default ("I believe" 0.10 mm) ([Bambu forum](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/don-t-know-how-to-setup-connectors-in-bambu-studio/35745)).
- Sliding: "standard sliding" 0.20–0.25 mm, "snug / alignment" 0.15–0.20 mm, "loose" 0.30–0.40 mm, on Prusa MK4 and XL (unstated) ([UF makerspace](https://makerspace.uflib.ufl.edu/services/3dprocess/recommended-software-for-3d-printing/designing-for-3d-printing-tolerances-on-the-mk4-and-xl/)). Fictiv: 0.4 mm for slide fits, 0.2 mm for tight fits (unstated) ([Fictiv](https://www.fictiv.com/articles/how-to-design-snap-fit-components)). Conflict: 0.20–0.25 vs 0.4 mm for sliding.
- Press fit: holes oversized "0.15mm-ish" for interference parts (on the diameter, so total) ([Prusa forum](https://forum.prusa3d.com/forum/original-prusa-i3-mk3s-mk3-hardware-firmware-and-software-help/adding-alignment-pins-in-slicr)). A press-fit table from Prusa's knowledge base: not found.
- Snap fits: Hubs 0.5 mm for FDM snap connectors (unstated) ([Hubs](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-snap-fit-joints-3d-printing)); Fictiv 0.3 mm for a "close fit snap joint" and 0.1–0.3 mm extra for printed snaps ([Fictiv](https://www.fictiv.com/articles/how-to-design-snap-fit-components)). Conflict: 0.5 vs 0.3 mm. BOSL2 defaults: `snap_pin()` clearance 0.2 mm, shrinking the pin from the socket walls (per side), preload 0.2 mm; `rabbit_clip()` clearance 0.1 mm added to socket width and length (total), compression 0.1 mm, socket about 0.4 mm deeper than the clip ([BOSL2](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/joiners.scad#module-rabbit_clip)).
- Holes: printed holes "almost always come out smaller than in CAD", and the top of a horizontal hole sags smaller ([CNC Kitchen](https://cnckitchen.com/blog/tips-and-tricks-for-heat-set-inserts)).

Snap-fit cantilevers

- Formulas: constant section, ε = 1.5·t·y / (L²·Q), with Q = 1 for a simple case ([Fictiv](https://www.fictiv.com/articles/how-to-design-snap-fit-components)). Bayer's design 2, with thickness tapering to half at the tip, gives y = 1.09·ε·L²/h and allows "more than 60%" more deflection than a constant section; tapering width to a quarter is the alternative ([Bayer MaterialScience guide](https://fab.cba.mit.edu/classes/S62.12/people/vernelle.noel/Plastic_Snap_fit_design.pdf), course-site copy).
- Strain limits, Bayer: for one brief snap, amorphous plastics up to about 70% of yield strain; materials with no distinct yield point about half the elongation at break; for frequent separation, about 60% of the single-snap value. Its table lists PC 4%, PC/ABS 2.5%, PC blends 3.5%, and no PLA or PETG ([Bayer](https://fab.cba.mit.edu/classes/S62.12/people/vernelle.noel/Plastic_Snap_fit_design.pdf)).
- Strain limits, Fictiv: PLA 4–8%, ABS 7%, nylon 4–15%; PETG not listed ([Fictiv](https://www.fictiv.com/articles/how-to-design-snap-fit-components)).
- PLA Pure, Bambu's product page: bending strength 75 MPa (XY) and 55 MPa (Z), bending modulus 2880 and 2599 MPa, impact strength 20.6 and 10.0 kJ/m² ([Bambu](https://us.store.bambulab.com/products/pla-pure)). Elongation at break isn't on that page, and Bambu's own PLA Pure data sheet: not found. A MatterHackers listing, which links a "TDS of Bambu PLA Pure" and gives test prints from a Bambu H2D, lists tensile strength 51.5 MPa (XY) and 30.0 MPa (Z), Young's modulus 2481 and 2196 MPa, and elongation at break 5.6% (XY) and 2.8% (Z) ([MatterHackers](https://www.matterhackers.com/store/l/bambu-lab-pla-pure-175mm-1kg/sk/M56EYX5E)).
- Conflict: Bambu says PLA Pure "matches Bambu PLA Basic in mechanical performance", while the same page lists lower impact strength than PLA Basic (20.6 vs 26.6 kJ/m² XY, 10.0 vs 13.8 Z) ([Bambu](https://us.store.bambulab.com/products/pla-pure)).
- PLA Pure strain budget (inference, arithmetic on the figures above): strength over modulus gives about 2.1% (tensile) to 2.6% (bending) in XY; Bayer's rules then give about 1.5–2.8% for one snap and 0.9–1.7% for repeated snaps. Conflict: that is well under Fictiv's 4–8% for PLA. For a hook that opens repeatedly, design to about 1.5%.
- Worked example (arithmetic, ε = 1.5%): a beam 10 mm long and 1.2 mm thick allows about 0.8 mm of undercut with Fictiv's formula, and about 1.4 mm with Bayer's half-taper.
- Root: fillet at least half the base thickness ([Hubs](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-snap-fit-joints-3d-printing)); Fictiv gives about half the wall thickness for molding ([Fictiv](https://www.fictiv.com/articles/how-to-design-snap-fit-components)); Bayer finds R/h of about 0.6 gives most of the benefit, never under 0.015 in (0.38 mm) ([Bayer](https://fab.cba.mit.edu/classes/S62.12/people/vernelle.noel/Plastic_Snap_fit_design.pdf)). Clip width 5 mm minimum; locating lugs share shear load ([Hubs](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-snap-fit-joints-3d-printing)).
- Return angle: separation force is found like mating force, using the return angle ([Bayer](https://fab.cba.mit.edu/classes/S62.12/people/vernelle.noel/Plastic_Snap_fit_design.pdf)); equal lead and return angles give equal assembly and removal force ([Fictiv](https://www.fictiv.com/articles/how-to-design-snap-fit-components)).

Print orientation

- Build cantilevers in the XY plane. Built along Z, elongation at break falls about 50% and tensile strength 20–30%, so halve the allowable strain ([Fictiv](https://www.fictiv.com/articles/how-to-design-snap-fit-components)); Hubs says to avoid Z-built cantilevers ([Hubs](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-snap-fit-joints-3d-printing)). PLA Pure's listed elongation agrees: 2.8% in Z against 5.6% in XY ([MatterHackers](https://www.matterhackers.com/store/l/bambu-lab-pla-pure-175mm-1kg/sk/M56EYX5E)).
- On a piece printed flat, a beam that lies in the face, flexing sideways or up, has layer lines along its length; a hook standing up from the face is Z-built (inference).
- BOSL2's `snap_pin()` defaults to "a printable configuration, flat side down", and double `rabbit_clip()`s to "the proper position for stand-alone printing" ([BOSL2 source](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/blob/master/joiners.scad)).

## Wearable pieces under strap load

- Strap anchors: a maker's Iron Man helper set includes a mount for 1.5 in (38 mm) nylon webbing, used with side-release buckles and triglides, glued or welded into the armor ([MyMiniFactory](https://www.myminifactory.com/object/3d-print-125951), 2020).
- "3D printed mounting points are the cleanest solution for straps"; "Velcro alone can fail. Straps alone can shift," so use both; shoulder pieces should put weight on the shoulders; plan anchors at design time ([Mandarin3D](https://mandarin3d.com/blog/3d-printing-for-cosplay-armor-props-and-accessories), vendor blog).
- Magnet latches: that maker's shin plates survive a swing "like a bat", "but if they catch on something, they can come apart" ([MyMiniFactory](https://www.myminifactory.com/object/3d-print-125951)).
- A stormtrooper kit maker: the armor parts "clamshell around your body with elastic straps", fastened with Velcro or rivets and button snaps, never attached to the bodysuit ([Studio Creations](https://www.studiocreations.com/howto/stormtrooper/st_assembly/index.html)).
- Heat: Bambu says not to expose PLA Pure parts to more than 60 °C ([Bambu](https://us.store.bambulab.com/products/pla-pure)); sun at outdoor events can warp PLA ([Mandarin3D](https://mandarin3d.com/blog/3d-printing-for-cosplay-armor-props-and-accessories)).
- The 405th forum's threads on attachment systems: blocked (403), not read.

## Tool support

BOSL2's `joiners.scad` has "Modules for joining separately printed parts including screw together, snap-together and dovetails" ([source](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/blob/master/joiners.scad)):

| Module | Meant for | Fit control |
|---|---|---|
| [`dovetail()`](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/joiners.scad#module-dovetail) | Male or female sliding dovetail, optionally tapered; `round=true` gives a puzzle-piece shape; `entry_slot_length` for a tail in mid-surface | `$slop` per side; `slope` default 6 |
| [`half_joiner()`, `half_joiner2()`](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/joiners.scad#functionmodule-half_joiner) | A matched pair of joiner halves, optional screw hole (`screwsize`); print orientation: not found | `$slop`, on `half_joiner()` only |
| [`joiner()`](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/joiners.scad#module-joiner) | A joiner that mates with a rotated copy of itself, optional screw hole | `$slop` |
| [`snap_pin()`, `snap_pin_socket()`](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/joiners.scad#module-snap_pin) | A separate split pin into sockets in two parts; presets (length × diameter) tiny 4 × 2.5, small 6 × 3.2, medium 8 × 4.6, standard 10.8 × 7 mm; `fixed=true` stops rotation | `clearance` 0.2, `preload` 0.10–0.20 by preset |
| [`rabbit_clip()`](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/joiners.scad#module-rabbit_clip) | A rectangular two-eared clip into a socket; "can be made at any height, so a suitable clip could be very thin"; types pin, socket, male, female, double; `lock` "may be irreversible" | `clearance` 0.1, `compression` 0.1 |
| [`half_of()`, `left_half()` and others](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/partitions.scad#functionmodule-half_of) | Cut a model in two along a plane with a pattern edge: jigsaw, dovetail, hammerhead, finger, comb, sawtooth and others | negative `offset` (docs examples -0.15, -0.5) |
| [`partition()`, `partition_cut_mask()`](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/partitions.scad#section-legacy-partitioning) | Legacy split into two mating parts, default cutpath "jigsaw"; deprecated for the `half_of()` family | `$slop` |

Bambu Studio's Cut tool:

- Modes: Planar (default) and Dovetail, with Depth, Width, Flap angle and Groove angle. Connectors: Plug, Dowel, Snap. It can cut to separate objects or to parts of one object ([Bambu wiki](https://wiki.bambulab.com/en/software/bambu-studio/cut-tool)).
- The source of release v02.08.02.61 (21 August 2026) adds a fourth connector, Thread ([GLGizmoAdvancedCut.cpp](https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/blob/v02.08.02.61/src/slic3r/GUI/Gizmos/GLGizmoAdvancedCut.cpp)). Conflict: the wiki lists three.
- Settings and defaults, from that source and [its header](https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/blob/v02.08.02.61/src/slic3r/GUI/Gizmos/GLGizmoAdvancedCut.hpp): Style Prism or Frustum; Shape Triangle, Square, Hexagon or Circle (Snap and Thread force Circle, Dowel forces Prism); Size 2.5 mm, a diameter; Depth ratio 3, which the code uses as the connector's height; each with a tolerance, default 0.1 mm. Snap adds Bulge 15% and Gap 30% (Gap 10–50%). Dovetail: Flap Angle 60° (30–120°), Groove Angle 0° (0–15°), Depth scaled to the model and Width 4 × Depth, each with a 0.1 mm tolerance.
- A Plug is part of one half; a Dowel prints as separate objects, with holes in both halves; connectors couldn't be rotated (forum, November 2023) ([Bambu forum](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/don-t-know-how-to-setup-connectors-in-bambu-studio/35745)). PrusaSlicer's tool has the same three types and a "Flip cut plane" to choose the plug side ([Prusa](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/cut-tool_1779)).

## Snap-on bezels and back plates

- A front bezel and back plate sandwich the insert. One template "intended for flat inserts such as photos, paper art, cardstock" holds it "with a friction fit" and a click ([Cuttle](https://cuttle.xyz/@bwente/3D-Printed-Snap-Fit-Photo-Frame-wFUjqowyrJq6)).
- Hooked lid: a student case used chamfered hooks into cutouts with 0.25 mm between hook and cutout top, which held "with no wiggle, without bowing"; PETG, with supports under the hook overhangs ([Fab Academy](https://fabacademy.org/2021/labs/charlotte/students/theodore-warner/Assignments/week05/), blog).
- Hooks standing up from a plate printed flat are Z-built, so halve the strain budget ([Fictiv](https://www.fictiv.com/articles/how-to-design-snap-fit-components)), and their overhangs may need supports, as in the case above. Support-free options (inference): pin the bezel to the wall with filament pins or flat-printed `snap_pin()`s; push double `rabbit_clip()`s, printed flat, into sockets in both parts; or make the latch a beam lying in the bezel's face that flexes sideways into a notch in the wall.
